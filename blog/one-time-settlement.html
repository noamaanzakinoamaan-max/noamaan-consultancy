How a Bank Loan One-Time Settlement (OTS) Works

By Noamaan Consultancy Services · Mumbai

If a loan has become impossible to repay and your account is heading towards — or already is — an NPA, you may not have to pay the full outstanding. A One-Time Settlement (OTS) lets you close the loan for a negotiated, reduced amount. Here's exactly how it works.

What is a One-Time Settlement?

An OTS is a formal agreement where the bank accepts a lump-sum payment that is less than the total outstanding, and in return marks the loan as settled and closed. Banks agree to this because recovering part of a bad loan quickly is often better for them than a long, uncertain legal recovery.

Who typically qualifies?

  • Borrowers whose accounts have turned into NPAs (usually 90+ days overdue).
  • Genuine financial hardship — business loss, medical emergency, income drop.
  • Cases where full repayment simply isn't realistic in the near term.

How the process works

  1. Review: We assess your loan, dues, and the bank's likely stance.
  2. Proposal: A settlement offer is prepared with supporting documentation of your hardship.
  3. Negotiation: We negotiate with the bank to bring the amount down to a fair, payable figure.
  4. Sanction letter: The bank issues an OTS approval with the agreed amount and timeline.
  5. Payment & closure: You pay as agreed and receive a No Due / settlement certificate.

Important things to know

A settled loan may be reported to credit bureaus as "settled" rather than "closed", which can affect your credit score for a period. We'll explain the trade-offs clearly so you make an informed choice — and, where possible, structure things to protect your future borrowing ability.
